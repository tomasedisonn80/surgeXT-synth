# Wave 8 / Agent 46 — independent research verification

**Review date:** 2026-11-14
**Scope:** All 16 linked evidence rows in [RESEARCH.md](../RESEARCH.md).
**Result:** Each row's factual claim is supported by its cited original interview, artist case study, official product documentation, or official technical manual. One narrow wording correction was applied to distinguish Hüttner's documented practice from the bank designer's inference. No preset, generator, DSP, packaging, or QA result was changed.

## Evidence boundaries

This is verification of 16 source pages, not a survey of 16 independent composers. The set contains 12 artist interviews, features, or case studies; three official instrument product pages; and one official synthesizer manual. Eight of the artist sources are vendor-hosted. Their firsthand statements can establish a particular artist's described practice, but vendor hosting and selective artist coverage limit their value for broad adoption claims.

The research appropriately separates documented practice, application to this bank, and limitations. It does not establish an industry-wide ranking, market share, universal genre suitability, or endorsement of these presets. “Best” and “most used” remain unproven at that level. Synthesized evocations of acoustic colors are not the sampled instruments or recorded performances in the cited scores. Technical render checks do not establish professional listening approval.

## Applied correction

In the Hüttner row's documented-practice column, “subtle moving textures can sustain documentary tension” was tightened to “subtle anxious sound design supports documentary scenes.” The preceding sequence statement now explicitly retains the condition that a project permits synthesizers. Low-intensity motion remains in the adjacent application column, where it is correctly identified as a design inference.

## Retrieval and access caveats

The original URLs and their subject/title correspondence were checked. The two Composer Magazine pages for Quayle and Hart repeatedly timed out on direct retrieval; the search service returned substantial indexed text from those same original publisher URLs, including the relevant interview answers. The Simko/Lee page initially exposed original content, and indexed original text supplied the remaining context after an intermittent follow-up failure. This verifies the cited claims but does not prove that all three pages' current live HTML was fully retrieved during this audit.

The remaining 13 sources were directly retrieved, including the Zimmer transcript PDF. No inaccessible page was silently replaced with a third-party summary. Product pages were used only for stated sound sources and features, not prices, purchasing advice, popularity, or artist endorsement.

Retrieval identifiers were supplied to the coordinating agent separately, allowing it to reopen the original evidence before citing it in the user-facing response. The durable citations here use the publishers' own URLs. All summaries are concise paraphrases; no extended quotations were copied.

## Conclusion

The source table is a defensible qualitative design rationale for a versatile synthesis bank. It cannot certify the user's stronger requests for the objectively best or most-used industry sounds, definite Hollywood adoption, or every-genre suitability. Those limits are already stated in the research and should remain visible in final release communication. No further factual correction was identified within the 16-row scope.
