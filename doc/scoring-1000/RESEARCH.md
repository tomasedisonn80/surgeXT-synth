# Scoring 1000: research and musical design

Research checked: 14 November 2026.

## What the evidence supports

Scoring 1000 is a collection of adaptable synthesis instruments organized around recurring scoring functions: intimate keys, articulated motifs, sustained harmony, atmosphere, low-frequency weight, melody, movement, and transitions. Composer interviews support these functions across films, television, and documentary work. They do not establish a statistically defensible ranking of the industry's “most used” sounds, and they cannot establish that any composer would definitely choose this new bank.

The research below prioritizes composers describing their own work, followed by official documentation of instruments developed with composers. This is a deliberately selected set of examples, not a representative survey. Public interviews omit many production decisions, and manufacturers select artists and products that serve their businesses. A vendor interview can provide credible first-person evidence of a particular technique while remaining weak evidence for market-wide adoption. Product pages establish the advertised design of a tool; they do not establish how frequently it appears in released scores.

The resulting design decisions are inferences from those sources. “Best” here means useful musical behavior, clear editing, sensible defaults, and a role within an arrangement. It is not an endorsement, an acoustic-realism rating, or a promise that one preset belongs in every genre.

Three principles recur in these accounts. First, a palette serves a particular story: soft keys can be more useful than a spectacular impact. Second, a recognizable family becomes flexible through performance, articulation, and processing. Third, acoustic and electronic elements often coexist. Those are design deductions from the examples above, not measured percentages of film music.

## The implemented collection

There are **200 authored synthesis families, each with five purposeful variants: 1,000 presets in ten categories of 100**. The equal allocation is a navigation and coverage decision, not an estimate of industry demand. Variants alter combinations of oscillator balance, spectral color, envelopes, motion, and spatial treatment. They are related instruments, not 1,000 independently invented synthesis architectures.

The [recipe contract](../../scripts/scoring/RECIPE_FORMAT.md), [source recipes](../../scripts/scoring/recipes), [builder](../../scripts/scoring/build_bank.py), and [catalog](manifest.json) make that structure inspectable. The builder initializes the native patch structure from Surge's CC0 Init Saw and applies the original recipe parameters; it does not import the cited commercial libraries or composers' presets.

| Category | Presets | Musical territory | Five variant names |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 01 Keys | 100 | Intimate keyboard attacks, electric and resonant key colors, harmonic sketches | Close, Felt, Glass, Float, Bite |
| 02 Plucks | 100 | Short motifs, ostinatos, string-like and electronic articulations | Close, Tender, Spark, Cascade, Shadow |
| 03 Bells and Mallets | 100 | Tuned impacts, metallic detail, woody resonance, magical accents | Close, Bloom, Glass, Alloy, Shadow |
| 04 Pads | 100 | Sustained harmony, emotional beds, slowly changing color | Close, Open, Bloom, Drift, Veil |
| 05 Ensemble Hybrids | 100 | Synthetic string, brass, and vocal-like layers and swells | Close, Section, Swell, Muted, Motion |
| 06 Drones and Air | 100 | Pedal tones, suspense, breath/noise layers, diffuse atmosphere | Near, Veiled, Open, Drift, Ripple |
| 07 Bass | 100 | Sub foundations, rounded bass, sharper rhythmic and melodic weight | Tight, Round, Edge, Pluck, Pulse |
| 08 Leads | 100 | Foreground melodies, lyrical voices, clear electronic lines | Near, Warm, Clear, Float, Phrase |
| 09 Pulses and Sequences | 100 | Repeating amplitude/filter motion and selected tuned pitch patterns | Soft, Drive, Sparse, Triplet, Glass |
| 10 Percussion and Transitions | 100 | Synthesized hits, noise accents, pitch gestures, swells and transitions | Close, Body, Edge, Drift, Bloom |

The underlying native oscillator models include Classic, Sine, FM2, FM3, Modern, and String. Oscillator combinations and envelopes supply the instrument identity; delay and reverb supply adjustable context. The collection needs no separately purchased samples or wavetables. Role descriptions, suggested MIDI ranges, tags, and variant intent are stored in the catalog and patch metadata.

Category 09 uses modulation patterns rather than a MIDI arpeggiator. Its explicitly tuned pitch-sequence families cover major pentatonic, minor pentatonic, root/fifth/octave, and whole-tone material. “Sparse” describes spaced accents and does not promise silent rests. Transition duration depends on playing and envelopes; a riser name does not guarantee an exact number of bars. These distinctions matter when fitting a cue to picture.

## Controls that support adaptation

The [builder's modulation assignments](../../scripts/scoring/build_bank.py) implement eight consistent macro labels. All are unipolar. Saved values start at zero except Space, which starts at 0.4 to recover the authored ambience.

| Macro | Implemented behavior | Musical use and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Tone | Opens a low/band-pass cutoff or lowers a high-pass cutoff; adds modest oscillator-color change | Adjust presence and available body without changing notes. |
| Motion | Increases LFO amplitude; also increases rate for unsynchronized motion | Intensify the existing movement. Zero retains authored motion; it is not an off switch. |
| Contour | Increases filter-envelope depth and decay; selected bright voices also shape amplitude decay and sustain | Reshape the transient and spectral evolution. |
| Space | Controls global delay and reverb mix | Zero is dry; higher values move the sound toward a more spacious treatment. |
| Color | Changes an oscillator-specific parameter, such as shape, FM amount, partial mix, or string stiffness | Alter the source character; the same knob need not produce identical timbre changes across models. |
| Width | Increases native stereo width and effect width | Adjust spread. Zero returns the authored width and is not a guaranteed mono setting. |
| Attack | Extends amplitude attack | Turn an immediate entrance into a slower onset; very short notes may become quieter. |
| Release | Extends amplitude release; also extends decay for short low-sustain instruments and relevant String decay | Lengthen a note's audible life without assuming every oscillator sustains indefinitely. |

Velocity controls level and filter brightness. The modulation wheel increases tone and motion; channel pressure introduces vibrato. These are deliberate performance mappings, not sampled articulation switching. A patch's default movement and width remain part of its authored identity. For exact stationary or mono behavior, edit the native routing/width or use the appropriate host processing and verify the result.

## Applying the bank across genres

The following are starting points for audition, not claims that genre suitability has been experimentally established. Arrangement, register, harmony, rhythm, and balance remain as important as the preset. Use the suggested range as a starting register, then check the actual voicing against the surrounding instruments.

| Context | Useful starting roles | Adaptation to try |
|---|---|---|
| Intimate drama or documentary | Close/Felt keys, Tender plucks, Veil pads | Reduce Space, play softer, leave room between phrases, and shorten tails around dialogue. |
| Suspense, crime, or horror | Veiled drones, sparse pulses, metallic accents | Restrict register, introduce Motion gradually, and contrast sustained tension with isolated attacks. |
| Action or thriller | Tight bass, Drive pulses, Edge percussion | Choose clear subdivisions, shorten overlapping notes, and reserve the broadest effects for punctuation. |
| Fantasy, animation, or wonder | Bells, mallets, Bloom pads, ensemble swells | Use contrasting registers and moderate Space; shape entrances with Attack and phrase endings with Release. |
| Science fiction or experimental music | FM colors, drifting drones, synthetic ensembles | Explore Color and pressure while keeping a stable harmonic or rhythmic reference where needed. |
| Pop, dance, hip-hop, or electronic tracks | Bass, plucks, leads, pulses | Set tempo first, choose the required envelope length, and use dry versions when a shared mix bus provides ambience. |
| Jazz, folk, rock, or acoustic hybrids | Warm leads, resonant keys, restrained pads | Treat synthesis as an additional instrumental color; reduce density and fit articulation to the performers. |

An ensemble-like pad might support an orchestral cue, an ambient record, or a pop chorus, but those uses require different voicings and balances. A wide, reverberant preset can be impressive alone yet obscure speech or consume the space needed by a real ensemble. Dry audition and a short representative cue are therefore useful musical decisions, not merely technical checks.

## Synthesis, realism, and the quality claim

“Piano,” “felt,” “string,” “brass,” “choir,” and similar labels describe an evoked color or role here. Native oscillators and filters can provide harmonic envelopes, resonances, breath-like noise, or ensemble-like beating. They do not contain recorded bows, breath articulations, consonants, piano microphone positions, or the performer variation of a sampled section. Compare the explicitly recorded preparations in the [Hauschka toolkit](https://www.spitfireaudio.com/products/hauschka-composer-toolkit) and the acoustic sources and velocity layers in [Contemporary Drama Toolkit](https://www.spitfireaudio.com/products/contemporary-drama-toolkit). A cue requiring convincing exposed orchestral or vocal performance may require recordings or a dedicated sampled instrument alongside this collection.

The automated workflow has five technical gates: native load identity, default audio integrity, performance range and dynamics, expression stress, and complete evidence fingerprints. Independent semantic serialization, sustained-note, diversity and package audits supplement those gates. Musical usefulness in a real cue still requires listening and judgment; it is not silently substituted for the fifth technical gate. Distinct parameter sets or different audio hashes do not, by themselves, establish perceptually valuable variation.

The [QA runner documentation](../../scripts/scoring/QA_RUNNER.md) and recorded reports define the actual measurements and their limits. This research document does not certify a listening pass for each preset. The generated catalog records musical audition separately; pending audition must remain pending until evidence supports a change. Technical checks cannot establish “Hollywood-approved” status, composer endorsement, acoustic realism, or universal artistic suitability. The defensible goal is a transparent, editable palette grounded in documented scoring practices, with measurable engineering quality and an honest musical review status.
