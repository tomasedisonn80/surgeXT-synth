### Spectral

The native hero is a smooth, approximately three-cycle folded sheet; the reference has numerous sharper irregular mountain-like ridges, a more visible lower mesh, a stronger selected-row pillar and brighter violet/cyan illumination. The difference is visually substantial.

With the deliberately simple image criterion max(R,G,B) ≥150 and max−min ≥40, bright/saturated coverage is 13.74% native versus 28.34% reference inside that plot. Mean RGB is approximately [13.4,25.7,63.4] native versus [26.8,33.8,105.0] reference. These are brightness diagnostics, not a fidelity score.

Correctable rendering priorities, ranked:

1. Increase emissive wire/glow and lower-plane grid visibility for large perspective plots while retaining original sample vertices.
2. Move large-perspective gradient stops toward stronger violet/magenta on the left and bright cyan on the right; avoid the broad dull blue middle.
3. Rebalance depth, camera and amplitude scaling/translation so the signed-sample surface reads as terrain above its lower sample-range plane.
4. Strengthen the actual selected-row marker's glow and contrast; its top must continue to follow a real sample.

Ridge count and sharpness are primarily a source-data issue. The current original audible Spectral Bloom table is the analytic `dunes` family: a dominant third harmonic with weaker fourth, first and seventh components. Projection cannot create extra genuine ridges. A different original periodic audible table can legitimately change this appearance because Surge supports the required native wavetable model; a display-only invented replacement cannot. Any such source revision requires regenerated provenance and audio/native verification.

### Aurora

The native hero bundles nearly parallel three-cycle traces into a narrow, dark band. The reference separates crossing lobes across a larger vertical/depth range and has broader luminous material. Bright/saturated coverage is 8.54% native versus 40.00% reference; mean RGB is [28.47,19.96,60.76] native versus [71.16,54.18,137.06] reference. The threshold's bounds are [295,327,1215,476] native versus [275,314,1223,487] reference.

Rendering can improve phase extent, frame-depth shear, strip opacity, halo width, color and brightness without changing source ordinates.
