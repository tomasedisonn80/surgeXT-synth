# Agent 59 / wave 10 — final CPU evidence analysis

Role: systems engineer and VST performance analyst. This was an independent,
read-only analysis of the completed experiment; no benchmark, compilation,
production edit, commit, push or additional agent was performed.

## Result and scope

The final experiment is valid under the recorded comparison contract, but it
does **not establish a broad reduction across workloads or the lowest possible
CPU consumption**. The three held effect fixtures have favorable CPU medians
and the strongest directional agreement in this run. Other scenarios remain
mixed, including an observed **5.008% higher median CPU time for `init-8`**.
That unfavorable result must remain visible.

The measured source checkpoint is
`489f0cf7bca2d3e129199b8779c7262a414d81f5`.
The experiment ran from **2026-11-15T12:05:37.681427+00:00** to
**2026-11-15T12:09:47.934944+00:00**, using eight pairs / 16 native invocations:
four baseline-first AB rounds and four candidate-first BA rounds. Each of ten
scenarios rendered 30 seconds held and five seconds after release at 48 kHz.
Both processes were pinned to logical CPU 0 on the reported shared AMD EPYC
9V74 Linux environment. Build and backend qualifications remain those in
[RESULTS](../RESULTS.md).

## Interpretation

- **Conditioner, EQ3 and EQ11 held processing:** observed CPU median reductions
  are 2.056%, 3.450% and 4.890%. Each fixture is faster in six of eight pairs, and
  both AB and BA subgroup paired medians are positive. This is the strongest
  directional support for benefit in this experiment. The overlapping ranges
  and paired reversals still limit confidence and transfer to other hosts,
  processors, backends, sample rates or presets.
- **EQ3 released processing:** the reported 8.282% reduction has seven favorable
  pairs and positive subgroup medians in both orders. It applies to the entire
  five-second released window, which can include idle processing; it is not an
  isolated coefficient-calculation benchmark.
- **`init-8` held processing:** the adverse 5.008% CPU median result is also
  present in wall time (5.007% higher). Only three of eight CPU pairs favor the
  candidate. However, the paired median is −6.539% in AB order and +0.516% in BA
  order, while the baseline and candidate CPU ranges span 25.7% and 21.4% of
  their respective medians. This run identifies an unfavorable result and an
  unresolved performance risk, but does not isolate a particular source
  change as its cause.
- **`unused-formula`:** the favorable 5.707% CPU ratio of medians has only four
  favorable pairs; the AB paired median is negative and the BA median is
  positive. It should not be used as a headline improvement. Formula-input
  skipping was rejected during implementation and was not introduced.
- **Other scenarios:** their mixed pair signs and order patterns do not support
  a general claim that every workload is faster. In particular, retain the
  negative idle and one-note held results and the active-formula released
  result rather than describing them as established neutral behavior.

Held CPU and wall reductions differ by at most about 0.86 percentage points.
The thread clock therefore does not erase or explain away the adverse wall
results as time spent descheduled. Remaining variability may reflect several
environment and execution effects; this experiment does not isolate their
causes. CPU charged to a Linux thread is not a hardware cycle count, a DAW CPU
meter, or a real-time deadline measurement.

No specific production correction is established by these diagnostics alone.
The evidence supports reporting bounded, observed fixture results and the
unresolved eight-note result. It does not support a speculative code change,
an unsupported universal CPU-saving percentage, or repeated measurements
selected until their signs become favorable. Establishing a stronger
cross-workload claim would require a controlled noise reference and isolated
change comparisons on an appropriate target, not reinterpretation of the
retained numbers.

Earlier wall-only experiments used a different implementation/harness checkpoint.
Their retention provides a truthful engineering history; comparison to this
run does not identify a causal speed contribution from the member relocation.

## Independent verification performed

I loaded and strictly revalidated all 16 native wall reports and all 16 CPU
sidecars, including metadata, scenarios, durations and audio-fingerprint
bindings. I recomputed every CPU summary exactly and every published wall
reduction from the raw arrays. The completed provenance has eight balanced
pairs, 16 successful child invocations and the recorded CPU affinity.

All five current harness/script hashes in provenance match the reviewed source.
The original runner reports complete first-pair PCM equality and identical
recorded fingerprints in all rounds. This review revalidated those report
bindings; it **did not reread the large PCM files** or rerun native processing.
Input-report SHA-256 hashes and the complete descriptive calculations are in
the linked independent JSON artifact.

This analysis is descriptive, with eight paired rounds on a shared machine.
No formal significance threshold or hardware-wide performance guarantee was
introduced after observing the results.
