All 20 baseline/candidate ranges (ten held and ten tail measurements) overlap.
Outside the active formula fixture, held medians last only about 7–118 ms of
actual timed execution. Within-build held ranges span 4.7–31.3% of their medians,
larger than the observed reported held changes. Range overlap alone is not a
formal significance test; these observations show that the available experiment
does not resolve small changes reliably.

The three effect fixtures each win only three of seven held pairs. Their paired
held medians are negative as well as their ratio-of-medians estimates. This is
not sufficient evidence of a reproducible regression, but it gives no basis for
advertising coefficient-cache speedups from this experiment. Init-16 and unused
formula win only two held pairs; retain those unfavorable observations too.

Order sensitivity is visible. Idle's paired held median is +6.585% in the four
AB rounds and −9.157% in the three BA rounds. Its ratio-of-medians estimate is
−2.195%, while the median paired reduction is +4.530%. Init-8 also changes sign
between these estimators. Seven alternating rounds contain four AB and three BA
pairs, so the order groups are not balanced. These are descriptive diagnostics,
not proof of a particular scheduler, cache or frequency cause.

No aggregate “overall CPU saving” should be formed from these ten arbitrarily
weighted fixtures. In particular, active PUC formula execution dominates total
wall time and is not representative of every preset or the LuaJIT plugin build.

## Fixture activation checks

`CpuBenchmark.cpp::configure()` loads each real effect through `setParameter01`,
processes the type change and checks the resulting effect pointer/type before
setting nontrivial coefficient inputs. All nine non-idle reports have nonzero
peaks in both builds. Conditioner, EQ3 and EQ11 fingerprints each differ from
`init-8`. The source and outputs therefore support exercising non-silent effect
fixtures; the near-zero timing differences should not be relabeled as proven
savings merely because coefficient work was removed in source.

The timer measures engine processing, preallocated PCM copies and transport.
The global FX slot is processed by the ordinary `SurgeSynthesizer::process()`
chain. There is no VST callback, host deadline, GUI, or standalone FX-wrapper
measurement in this executable. Shared hardware and frequency variation remain
uncontrolled factors.

## Bounded next measurement

Recommend one predeclared follow-up using **eight rounds** (four AB and four BA),
**30 seconds held** and **five seconds after release**, with all ten scenarios at
48 kHz. Freeze the binaries, preserve the original report, run without concurrent
builds or heavy local tests, and retain every raw timing and all results. This
increases actual timed held work by ten times and balances execution order while
remaining within the current native CLI and child timeout bounds.

The longer tail is a different fixture window and will include more idle work
for patches with short releases. Label it as a five-second post-release result;
do not directly compare its median reduction with the one-second pilot as if
only measurement precision changed. The longer held period also weights steady
state more heavily. It is appropriate for the unchanged-parameter coefficient
cache, with startup/lifecycle correctness covered by the separate regressions.

After reviewing that fixed follow-up, a matched 96 kHz experiment can check
whether any observed effect survives a rate change. Preserve and report negative
and inconclusive results. Do not repeat until a favorable run appears or remove
outliers without a predeclared, externally supported reason. If small changes
remain unresolved, report the uncertainty and reserve a universal speed claim
until isolated-machine profiling or hardware measurements can distinguish it.

No hypothesis test or confidence interval is asserted here: seven serial pairs
on shared hardware are too weak a basis for assuming independent, identically
distributed timings or for claiming all deployment targets improve.
